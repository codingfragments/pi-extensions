# UX19 report: map zoom and the picture at each anchor

Slice UX19 (leftovers grill): the two niceties that were on the "never built" list since the first map.

## 1. Map zoom and Fit (D132)

The map's toolbar has **−**, **Fit** and **+**, with the level shown beside them.

![Fit: the whole graph in a small window](../images/ux/map-fit-dark-1200.jpg)

- **Fit** picks the zoom at which the whole graph is in the window (never above 100 %, never below 30 %). The
  picture shows a 1200 x 640 window at 48 %.
- **Keys:** `+` `−` `0` (zoom in, zoom out, fit) when you are not typing in a field. All of it is in the `?` sheet.
- **Reset view** goes back to 100 %. Zoom is a view setting: not in the address, and it never changes which versions are shown.
- The scaled graph is laid out by the browser (CSS `zoom`), so scrolling, the sticky stage headers and clicking a node
  still work when zoomed (a test clicks a node at the Fit level).
- **Mouse:** the wheel zooms at the pointer (one notch is one step; a pinch zooms smoothly), and dragging the background pans
  (see "Mouse: wheel and pan" below). **Not built:** a minimap.

## 2. The picture at each anchor in the Voice inspector (D133)

Under the narration of a section, one card per `[@mark]` it speaks: the keyframe the take's analysis took at that mark,
the time into the take, and a line of what the page said then.

![The picture at each anchor](../images/ux/voice/voice-anchors-dark-1400.jpg)

- In spoken order, once each. A section without anchors (an intro, an outro) shows nothing.
- Where the analysis has no keyframe for a mark the card says "no picture for this moment" and still shows the page text.
- The server only offers files inside the project. It renders nothing itself: the keyframes are the ones the take's
  analysis already wrote.

## Fix after the first release: zoomed in, groups broke the boxes

At 120 % and above, opening or collapsing a group made the boxes far too tall. The map measures how tall each box really
draws (so wrapped titles and badges get room) with `getBoundingClientRect`, and with CSS `zoom` that returns the *scaled*
size: every re-layout read 1.6 times the real height as the box's height and stored it, so boxes grew with each change.
At 100 % or less the measure never exceeded the default, which is why it only showed zoomed in.

- The measure now divides by the scale it finds (the stage's drawn width over its layout width), so it is the same at
  any zoom and in any browser that does or does not scale the readings.
- Zooming now keeps the middle of the view in the middle (before, zooming in at the top left left you looking at empty
  space), and quick repeats of a key add up.
- `test/ux19-map-zoom.test.ts` opens a group and collapses all of them at 120 % and 160 % and asserts every box has exactly
  its 100 % height; it fails without the fix. Another assertion keeps the middle of the view in place.

## Mouse: wheel and pan

- **The wheel zooms, at the pointer:** the point of the graph under the pointer stays under the pointer (the keys and the
  buttons zoom around the middle of the view). One notch is one zoom step; a pinch (Ctrl + small deltas) zooms smoothly.
- **Shift + wheel, and a sideways scroll, still scroll** the view, so the wheel does not take scrolling away: it moves to
  Shift + wheel, the scrollbars, and dragging.
- **Dragging the background pans.** It starts only on the background: not on a version, a button, a link or the scrollbars,
  so selecting and pressing work as before. A press that moves less than 4 px is a click and does nothing. The cursor is a
  hand (`grab`, then `grabbing` while you drag); the mouse only (touch keeps its native scrolling). Panning never changes
  the address.
- Both are in the `?` sheet ("Mouse in the Map").

Evidence: `test/map-pointer.test.ts`: the point under the pointer stays put at 120 % and at 160 %; wheel down zooms out;
Shift + wheel and a sideways scroll do not zoom; a pinch is a small step; the background shows a grab cursor; a click
without moving changes nothing; a drag scrolls the other way and shows `grabbing`; a drag that starts on a version does
not pan, and a click on it still selects.

## Trackpad: pinch and two-finger pan, independent of the wheel

A trackpad and a mouse wheel both send `wheel` events, so the map tells them apart (`wheel-kind.js`, a pure module with its own
unit tests) instead of treating every scroll as a zoom:

| What arrives | What it is | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ctrl + wheel (what a pinch sends in Chrome, Edge and Firefox) | a pinch | zooms at the fingers, smoothly; in every mode |
| Safari's `gesturestart` / `gesturechange` (a cumulative scale) | a pinch | zooms at the fingers by the change of scale |
| Shift + wheel | a pan | the browser scrolls it |
| sideways movement, a fractional delta, or a small first delta | two fingers | **pans**: the browser scrolls it, with its inertia; never a zoom |
| a delta of a full notch (100, 120) or a line / page delta | a mouse wheel | zooms at the pointer |

The first event of a gesture decides and the rest of that burst keeps the decision (events closer than 60 ms), so a
trackpad scroll that grows to notch size does not turn into a zoom half way. Panning by two fingers is left to the browser
on purpose: it is smooth, has inertia and respects the system's scroll direction.

**The guess can be wrong on some device** (a mouse wheel with a smooth, accelerated scroll sends small deltas). The foot of the map
has **Wheel: Auto / Zooms / Scrolls**: *Zooms* makes every plain wheel event zoom, *Scrolls* makes every one scroll; a pinch
and Shift + wheel are the same in all three. The choice is remembered in this browser.

Evidence: `test/map-trackpad.test.ts`: the classifier for each row of the table, the burst rule and the override; in the browser,
a two-finger scroll pans and does not zoom, a fractional delta is left to the browser (not prevented), a pinch zooms and the point
between the fingers stays put, Safari's gesture events zoom by their scale, and the override changes what the wheel does and is
remembered across a reload. **Not tested on real hardware:** the browser tests send the same events a trackpad sends, but nobody
has tried a physical trackpad or a physical Safari yet; the override is there for that reason.
